# Power Atlas — evidence-linked redistricting research

Power Atlas is a public, low-storage research index connecting redistricting cases, public officials, campaign committees, congressional elections, judicial appointments and geographic boundaries. **A source-indexed record is not a verified allegation of influence or misconduct.**

## Location-first: how power affects everyday life (November 6, 2026)

The new [community and everyday-life explorer](platform/unified/my-life.html) shifts the reader's entry point from institutions to nine lived issues: housing, groceries, utilities, healthcare, jobs/pay, schools/childcare, transportation, taxes/services and representation. The [unified homepage](platform/unified/index.html) now leads with that community-based journey while retaining the existing research dashboard.

The journey has four steps:

1. **Where:** User-submitted five-digit U.S. ZIP; optional browser-side third-party postal-place lookup from Zippopotam.us. ZIPs are not exact addresses, political boundaries, utility service areas or school districts. ZIP values are not recorded by Power Atlas, used to infer political preferences, or included in shared URLs.
2. **Everyday issue:** [Nine versioned, sourced-action and mechanism narratives](data/place-impact/life-issues-v1.json) explain relevant authorities and market mechanisms without inventing personal price or benefit calculations.
3. **Evidence:** [Source-by-issue manifest](data/place-impact/deep-resource-map-v1.json) joins **all 25 existing program IDs** from [the canonical inventory](data/research/atlas-inventory-v1.json), **nine recent datasets** (corporate PACs, federal contract records, political donation research, public oversight, holding data and reforms), and **37 upstream primary, nonprofit, journalistic and academic source directories**. The website reads the canonical inventory at runtime, including its original coverage status, evidence link, and missing-data explanation. Topic relevance is editorial, never a claim of local causation.
4. **Action:** Official programs and neutral civic pathways, with separate examples of public policy options and their trade-offs. The platform does not endorse or rank politicians, political parties, candidates, campaigns, policy options or individuals.

**Current data limitation:** The local evidence layer now uses the independent, free Census Reporter service to request actual 2020–2024 ACS 5-year ZCTA estimates on ZIP submission, without a Census API key, as documented in the next section. Data are historical community estimates, not current grocery shelf prices, private utility bills, electoral districts or donation-to-contract-to-household causal joins. Browser access and ZCTA availability can differ by location; unavailable values are not replaced with guesses. The 37 source directories are not claims of complete ingestion, licensing or verified influence.

Validation: [place/deep-research tests](tests/test_place_impact.py), [Census ZCTA measures](tests/test_local_issues.py), [national/state metric and evidence connections](tests/test_connected_daily_life.py), and the [free CI workflow](.github/workflows/validate-place-impact.yml) verify issue completeness, source integrity, privacy boundaries, and browser JavaScript syntax.

## Source-backed local measurements across all nine everyday issues (November 6, 2026)

The [resident-first browser experience](platform/unified/my-life.html) now uses two distinct layers of evidence for **every** Step 2 category, in addition to the original [34 deep research entries](data/place-impact/deep-resource-map-v1.json):

- **Historical local measurements:** [18 audited Census ZCTA measures](data/place-impact/local-measures-v1.json), two per category: housing (rent, home value), groceries (SNAP, poverty), utilities (electric/gas heating fuel), healthcare (uninsured share/count), work (household income, unemployment), schools (school/preschool enrollment), transport (public transit/work-from-home commuting), public services (no-internet households/rate), and representation (population, median age). Observations are from **2020–2024 ACS 5-year** detailed tables, loaded by Census Reporter on demand using the visitor-submitted five-digit ZIP as a candidate Census **ZCTA**. ZCTAs are **not** identical to USPS delivery areas, nor are they valid school, electoral, government, or utility jurisdiction identifiers. Each card links its exact official Census table and states what the figure cannot establish.
- **Current/historical state and national context:** [Verified public statistics](data/place-impact/connected-metrics-v1.json) include BLS/FRED index snapshots for housing, groceries, medical care and transportation, national federal expenditures, public-school spending, national and **51-state/DC unemployment**, **51 state/DC all-sector electricity prices**, and a documented 10-state sample of school spending. The location label comes from an optional external ZIP lookup, not an exact district. State metrics are shown only for actual source-covered states; national metrics never masquerade as local.
- **Connected official decisions and funding:** Step 3 displays source-backed, clearly scoped links to [state election authorities](data/elections/official-source-index.json), secondary voting-rule classifications requiring official statute verification, Treasury [verified recipient contract actions](data/business-influence/federal-contract-transactions-v1.json), [eight corporate/PAC research candidates](data/business-influence/additional-candidates-v1.json) and separately labeled [proposed political-money reforms](data/money-influence/civic-power-reforms-v1.json). These are relevant records to inspect, **not** proved donors influencing policy or household expenses.
- **Successful nine-issue API smoke test:** [Source health snapshot](data/place-impact/local-source-health-v1.json) records two real, externally verified ZCTA estimates for each of nine topics, for **fixed sample ZCTA 10001 only**, never a visitor's entered ZIP. Source requests and timestamps are auditable and partial/failed runs preserve the prior snapshot. The [zero-cost source-audit workflow](.github/workflows/verify-local-sources.yml) runs the exact 2024 Census Reporter release without a private API key.
- **Data gaps remain substantive:** No source-verified ZIP-to-school-district or legislative-district boundaries, precise local officials, retail basket/energy invoices, childcare fees, school quality, municipality tax receipts, or causal donation-to-policy-to-household impact joins are claimed. Future source additions require exact jurisdictions and dated policy events, not a guess from ZIP or party.

The interface uses one public API request per selected issue and entered ZIP, with an optional same-vintage U.S. comparison. It never stores the ZIP or infers political affiliation. Zippopotam.us and Census Reporter are independent third parties which see the ZIP on opt-in submission, so source privacy terms apply. A blocked response displays unavailable—not fictitious zeroes. Regression coverage: [all nine metric calculations](tests/test_local_issues.py), [place and deep research map](tests/test_place_impact.py), and the GitHub workflow [validate-place-impact](.github/workflows/validate-place-impact.yml).

- The graph flags possible **sequential payment leads** only when there are two *actual* filing-backed edges joining the same verified entity, within 45 days and within 5% of the incoming amount. Such a pattern is not proof the same dollars moved, misconduct, or shared ownership. **Fuzzy names, matching vendors, registered agents and GNN output never create direct financial edges.**

Users can switch votes from raw counts to within-party share, rescale period squares by roll-call volume and set a transparent, non-normative strict-opposition threshold. The effect of the control is stated beside each visualization.
- [Research catalog](data/research/atlas-inventory-v1.json) centrally inventories **25 themes**, including missing or incomplete tracks for 2018–2026 voter coalitions, competitive-race issue polling, congressional stock transactions, wealth/price effects, historical party-policy effects, and political-action classification. These are **coverage labels and next-evidence needs**, not claims that the missing data were ingested or that those hypotheses are true.
- The default [unified landing page](platform/unified/index.html) now promotes the comparison studio. The separate [research frontend](site/index.html) routes readers toward the canonical unified evidence explorer.
- Design reference: [Extremists on Both Sides](https://both-sides.the7eye.org.il/en/), with one-square-per-observation, configurable scale and filters; political-party identity is not an extremism rating. Comparing an itemized receipt to an investigative lead or an unverified original donor requires **different denominators** and they are **never combined**.

## Mobile research-map usability refinement (November 5, 2026)

The [comparison studio](platform/unified/compare.html) now uses a two-column mobile topic map instead of 25 expanded research-memo cards. Its evidence coverage graphic summarizes the 25 topics by status, category filtering and keyword search remain available, and each topic opens an accessible, closable detail dialog with its research question, explicit evidence gap, and original-source link. Status totals are derived from the catalog rather than hardcoded as universal completion claims. The desktop layout uses four tiles per row, with a three-column intermediate breakpoint.

Mobile navigation uses four compact visible labels; voting coalitions remain source-backed and the campaign-money section uses three short but distinctly classified evidence stages. Longer methodological notes remain available via expandable explanations. This is a visual/readability change, not evidence ingestion, new entity attribution, a causality claim, or a statement that every topic is complete.

Validation: run python -m unittest discover -s tests -p test_story_atlas.py -v and the existing broad test suite. Publish to Vercel from the Git commit explicitly; repository changes do not, by themselves, update the live site.

## Cross-domain source-backed observations — November 5, 2026

- [Unified public evidence panel](platform/unified/compare.html#people) visualizes existing 2025 selected S&P 500 holdings compilations (508 readable reports; 178 reporting direct shares/options; 43 paper reports not parsed), a stale and nonrepresentative 58-row 2026 Senate transaction delta from only three filers (spouse/self separate), and two unweighted ANES 2024 political-efficacy frequency tables. These different observational grains do not establish politician misconduct, net worth or electoral causation.
- [Consolidated, source-attributed machine-readable evidence](data/research/cross-domain-evidence-v1.json) is derived from three already-reviewed pilot documents in `data/research/political-authenticity/` and includes explicit source links and limits. The source evidence is *not* independently re-audited against individual original filings.
- [Official Cooperative Election Study download catalog](https://tischcollege.tufts.edu/research-faculty/research-centers/cooperative-election-study/data-downloads) includes public 2018, 2020, 2022 and 2024 survey datasets; [2024 common-content release](https://doi.org/10.7910/DVN/X11EP6) is listed as CC0. Their respondent-level data are **source discovered, not imported** into Power Atlas; no demographic voting-influence findings or competitive-district issue models are asserted from the catalog.
- The `congress-trades` topic is now labeled **partial evidence**, replacing the incorrect blanket `needs_data` label. Survey comparison topics remain flagged as unmodeled because raw respondent-level records and required weight/coding harmonization have not been integrated.
