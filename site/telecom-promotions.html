1Where the promised perks go

A two-line More account gets four Picks in total. One advertised up-to-$1,200 phone offer consumes three—leaving just one for the other line and everything else.

What a headline phone deal leaves out

  • Promo lock: Up-to-$1,200 or $930 credits arrive across 36 months, subject to continued eligibility.
  • Early payoff: The published offer terms say future bill credits end when the financed device is paid off early.
  • Plan limits: T-Mix has no phone-line price guarantee; account changes can affect benefit availability.
  • Switching costs: Existing loyalty or home-internet bundle discounts can change when mobile lines move—check those separately.

Promotional credit does not erase your remaining device balance if a required condition stops being met.

4How to avoid getting surprised

Before checkout

Demand an account-wide quote

Have the carrier show both lines, all shared Picks, both device installments, each promotional credit, monthly fees, and the point at which credits stop. Compare household totals, not a single “free phone” headline.

When a promise doesn't match

Save the evidence

Keep dated ad and checkout screenshots and the written terms. Ask the provider to correct discrepancies. The FCC's consumer complaint center accepts wireless billing complaints; filing does not itself prove a violation.

What is proven—and what isn't

Verified structure

Shared account-level Picks, the four-Pick two-line More allowance, two- and three-Pick promotion requirements, and published 36-month conditions are supported by T-Mobile's public documents.

Not established

That any specific household was deceived, that every eligible couple is denied a promised promotion, or that the structure is unlawful. A real account's eligibility and final bill require a carrier quote and bill checks.
