Research charter

What is — and isn't — established

This platform publishes source-linked references, not accusations. Litigation listings do not establish a judgment. Committee names do not prove relationships. Aggregate receipts and disbursements do not identify a beneficiary or influence over a map.

Reported committee payments

Judicial appointments / Federal Judicial Center

Who appointed the judges?

Historical Article III judicial appointments from the Federal Judicial Center: court, appointing president, nomination, Senate confirmation and commission dates. An appointment is a documented institutional relationship—not evidence of a ruling's motive or improper influence.

District election outcomes

U.S. House party vote totals

MIT Election Data and Science Lab source; compact state-year totals. These are not gerrymandering scores. Uncontested districts, changing boundaries, and third-party votes limit comparisons.

State-level observed seat-versus-vote figures are shown only for datasets with both major parties reporting votes in every indexed district and without known missing unopposed returns. These are descriptive—not gerrymandering scores.

Data source: MIT Election Data and Science Lab U.S. House 1976-2024 ↗, CC0 1.0. Source-specific validation is pending. Neither a vote margin nor a partisan advantage alone proves improper district design.

Source evidence policy

Research records are published with source URLs, review statuses, stable identifiers and coverage notes. A tentative name match never proves causation or misconduct.

Directly attributable filings are kept distinct from inferred networks. Unreviewed relationships should not be used as allegations.
