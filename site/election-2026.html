Three questions the results can answer

1. Did the maps deliver?

Compare targeted districts' actual winners with the mapmakers' intended partisan advantage. Track close and uncalled races separately.

2. Were seats representative?

Compare district-level votes with seats won. A national vote-seat difference alone does not establish gerrymandering or an efficiency gap.

3. Who controls the next maps?

Trace governors, legislative chambers, commissions, courts and ballot measures — with a separate rule for each state's mapmaking process.

What to verify before calling this settled

  • Official state canvasses, certification dates, recounts and runoffs.
  • District-level returns matched to the exact map version used in 2026.
  • Precinct-level comparison or neutral-map simulations to separate national swing from map design.
  • Party control after all legislative races are called, with commission and judicial constraints.

Research caution: preliminary projections and analysis are not certified results. The companion research contains conflicting state-officeholder claims and unverified efficiency-gap estimates; these are intentionally excluded from the headline figures until independently checked.
