### US 2020: Medicare negotiation and senior drug expenses

The [17 August 2020 Democratic platform](https://www.presidency.ucsb.edu/documents/2020-democratic-party-platform), *Bringing Down Drug Prices and Taking on the Pharmaceutical Industry*, promised Medicare negotiating power **for public and private purchasers** and a cap on seniors' out-of-pocket prescription drug costs. These are separate atomic candidate commitments from the same passage, attributed to the party rather than personally to the nominee.

The [Inflation Reduction Act, Public Law 117-169](https://www.govinfo.gov/app/details/PLAW-117publ169), was signed **16 August 2022** and is already indexed in the prior ledger. [CMS selected the first 10 Part D drugs on 29 August 2023](https://www.cms.gov/newsroom/press-releases/statements-cms-leadership-historic-announcement-first-selected-drugs-medicare-drug-price-negotiation). [CMS announced negotiated prices on 15 August 2024](https://www.cms.gov/newsroom/press-releases/negotiating-lower-drug-prices-works-saves-billions), **effective 1 January 2026**, after the 2021–25 term. This narrower selected-Medicare-drug authority does **not** demonstrate all-public-and-private-purchaser reach; CMS' estimated hypothetical savings are **not realized fiscal observations**.

[CMS confirmed on 10 January 2025](https://www.cms.gov/newsroom/press-releases/cms-releases-proposed-2026-payment-policy-updates-medicare-advantage-and-part-d-programs) that the **$2,000 Part D annual out-of-pocket cap took effect 1 January 2025** (indexed to $2,100 in 2026). This is limited to covered Part D drugs and enrollees, not all prescriptions for all seniors. No outcome verdict is assigned.

### US 2024: a necessary negative match check

[Public Law 119-21, section 70202](https://www.govinfo.gov/content/pkg/PLAW-119publ21/html/PLAW-119publ21.htm), enacted **4 July 2025**, creates a **qualified overtime premium income-tax deduction**, not a universal exclusion of all overtime pay or payroll tax. It is capped at **$12,500** for individuals and **$25,000** for joint filers, subject to income phaseouts, for tax years **2025–2028**. No dated, attributable pre-election 2024 campaign text has been confirmed for this particular claim within the frozen source manifest; **do not count it as a matched pledge**. [IRS guidance](https://www.irs.gov/newsroom/what-to-know-about-the-no-tax-on-overtime-deduction) explains the narrowed scope.

### UK 2024: abolition versus policy inheritance

The [2024 Labour manifesto](https://labour.org.uk/change/strong-foundations/) pledged to end non-dom tax status and replace it with limited relief for short-term UK residents. The [Finance Act 2025](https://www.legislation.gov.uk/ukpga/2025/8/enacted), granted Royal Assent **20 March 2025**, section 40 and Schedule 9, ended the remittance basis from tax year 2025–26. [HMRC guidance](https://www.gov.uk/hmrc-internal-manuals/international-manual/intm603665) confirms **6 April 2025** operation with four-year foreign-income-and-gains relief and legacy transition rules. **Important competing explanation:** the preceding Conservative government [announced a similar abolition in Spring Budget 2024](https://www.gov.uk/government/publications/changes-to-the-taxation-of-non-uk-domiciled-individuals/technical-note-changes-to-the-taxation-of-non-uk-domiciled-individuals). Enactment cannot be attributed exclusively to Labour's campaign. The separate offshore-trust pledge needs atomization.

### Germany 2025: existing pledge, newly dated implementation

The [CDU/CSU pre-election programme](https://www.cdu.de/wahlprogramm-von-cdu-und-csu/) proposed an Aktivrente allowing up to **€2,000/month** tax-free for qualifying work beyond normal retirement age. This pledge was already in the 30 October corpus. The [official promulgated law, BGBl 2025 I No. 361](https://www.bundesfinanzministerium.de/Content/DE/Gesetzestexte/Gesetze_Gesetzesvorhaben/Abteilungen/Abteilung_IV/21_Legislaturperiode/2025-11-07-G-Aktivrente/4-Verkuendetes-Gesetz.pdf?__blob=publicationFile&v=2), dated **22 December** and published **23 December 2025**, took effect **1 January 2026**. [Federal Finance Ministry guidance](https://www.bundesfinanzministerium.de/Content/DE/FAQ/FAQ-zur-Aktivrente.html) confirms the income-tax exemption and exclusions for self-employed people and civil servants. This establishes legal operation, **not** claimant counts, revenue loss or behavioral impact. Coalition negotiations and legislative changes remain relevant.

### Actual US fiscal observations around the 2024 election

The official [Treasury Monthly Treasury Statement for January 2025](https://fiscaldata.treasury.gov/static-data/published-reports/mts/MonthlyTreasuryStatement_202501.pdf), **Table 1**, reports total federal outlays, in **millions of current USD**, for October–January in both fiscal years:

| Calendar month | FY2024 comparison | Calendar month | FY2025 |
|---|---:|---|---:|
| October 2023 | 469,997 | October 2024 | 584,220 |
| November 2023 | 588,842 | November 2024 | 668,517 |
| December 2023 | 558,665 | December 2024 | 541,146 |
| January 2024 | 499,250 | January 2025 | 641,935 |

These **8 observations** are actual total federal modified-cash-basis outlays, not budget proposals or estimates. November 2024 includes the **5 November presidential election**, but month-level aggregation cannot isolate election-day timing. Treasury explicitly warns about payment-date acceleration and rounding. Total outlays combine transfer programs, interest, operations and investment; **they cannot serve as a long-term-investment or electoral-targeted-transfer share**. These federal data are not harmonized with the existing 650 US state fiscal-year observations or Canadian departmental actuals. The January 2025 report fixes the vintage; later revisions must be logged.

## Research gates and limitations

1. **Denominator:** the campaign-document universe, all eligible clauses, excluded/duplicated clauses, version hashes and cohort membership are **not frozen**. These three outcome-guided examples are not a random or complete sample. Zero population-level rates.
2. **Review:** two genuinely independent human coders must review source passages, atomization, governing-party attribution, deadlines, outcome scope, competing evidence and disputed interpretations. No automated CI run counts as an independent coder.
3. **Time:** US Medicare prices applicable in 2026 must not be retroactively counted as delivered during the 2021–25 term. Record enactment, selection, announcement, price operation and measured beneficiary effect separately.
4. **Fiscal:** for an election-cycle model, harmonize definitions and vintages, identify ex-ante election clocks, construct properly defined targeted transfer and capital outlay components, control for payment-date shifts and seasonality, and test alternatives. Do not infer causal opportunism from these eight months.
5. **Rights and verification:** party campaign texts remain linked, not bulk reproduced. US federal and UK/German official laws are cited with precise locators; publication/reuse rights and original-source hashes need review. UK and German implementation guidance may be updated after its stated effective date.
6. **Coverage:** Canada and Portugal retain their previously indexed source/candidate evidence; no new record for either is claimed in this increment. Canadian one-dollar departmental table/graph discrepancy remains unresolved. Germany's existing pledge is not duplicated.
7. **Separation:** no edits to redistricting ingestion, site, prior research ledger or fiscal model outputs. No paid API calls, authentication workarounds or unsupported fulfillment scores.

**CI validates internal structure, not the underlying empirical truth.**
