## Pre-election pledge → action → durability

The [2020 Democratic platform, Climate Change section](https://www.presidency.ucsb.edu/documents/2020-democratic-party-platform) promises re-entry into the Paris Agreement. The [January 20, 2021 presidential acceptance instrument](https://www.govinfo.gov/content/pkg/DCPD-202100049/html/DCPD-202100049.htm) documents the action; [the State Department](https://2021-2025.state.gov/the-united-states-officially-rejoins-the-paris-agreement/) identifies **February 19, 2021** as re-entry into force. The [January 20, 2025 executive order](https://www.govinfo.gov/content/pkg/DCPD-202500129/html/DCPD-202500129.htm) directed withdrawal. The [UN depositary notification dated January 27, 2025](https://treaties.un.org/doc/Publication/CN/2025/CN.71.2025-Eng.pdf) specifies **January 27, 2026** as the effective withdrawal date, corroborated by [current UN treaty status](https://treaties.un.org/doc/Publication/MTDSG/Volume%20II/Chapter%20XXVII/xxvii-7-d.en.xml). These dates establish an observable **policy reversal**, not the cause of that reversal, actual climate impact, or a human-reviewed fulfillment outcome. Formal re-entry during the 2021–2025 term and post-term durability must be separate variables.

The [2024 Republican platform, Chapter 3, item 2](https://www.presidency.ucsb.edu/documents/2024-republican-party-platform) expressly refers to making the increased standard deduction and expanded Child Tax Credit permanent. [Public Law 119-21, enacted July 4, 2025](https://www.govinfo.gov/content/pkg/PLAW-119publ21/html/PLAW-119publ21.htm), §70102 removes the sunset and increases standard deduction amounts (applicable to tax years beginning after December 31, 2024). Section 70104 removes the expanded CTC sunset and raises the stated amount to $2,200, **with Social Security number restrictions** and other conditions (same tax-year effective boundary). Neither provision proves net household savings, universal eligibility, or independently adjudicated fulfillment. Reconciliation rules and legislative control are relevant mechanisms, not evidence of personal motives.

## Actual Treasury data and identification warnings

The [January 2021 MTS, Table 1](https://fiscal.treasury.gov/files/reports-statements/mts/mts0121.pdf) reports total federal outlays (USD millions): September 2020 **497,780** (FY2020), October **521,769**, November **364,819**, December **489,682**, and January 2021 **547,483** (the last four FY2021). November contains the November 3 presidential election. The report itself flags December payment-date accelerations and approximately **$139 billion** of January 2021 economic impact payments. Pandemic response, timing, fiscal-year boundaries and seasonality preclude attributing fluctuations to electoral opportunism. These are total outlays, **not** capital spending, discretionary transfers, appropriations, or a comparable general-government GFCF measure.
