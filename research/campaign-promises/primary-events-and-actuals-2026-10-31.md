### US 2020 pre-election commitments and documented official actions

The [Democratic platform of 17 August 2020](https://www.presidency.ucsb.edu/documents/2020-democratic-party-platform), section **Strengthening the U.S. Postal Service**, separately commits to pursuing repeal of the retiree health prefunding mandate and maintaining six-day service. [Public Law 117-108](https://www.govinfo.gov/app/details/PLAW-117publ108), approved **6 April 2022**, reforms the prefunding mandate and codifies six-day delivery. In its [10 November 2022 FY2022 report](https://about.usps.com/newsroom/national-releases/2022/1110-usps-reports-fiscal-year-2022-results.htm), USPS confirmed prefunding repeal and a **$57.0 billion noncash accounting benefit**. That number is **not** expenditure, cash savings, or the measured effect of the campaign pledge. USPS advocated the reform before the election ([7 February 2020 USPS statement](https://about.usps.com/newsroom/statements/020720-hr-2382-usps-fairness-act.htm)); legislative sponsorship and bipartisan agreement are competing explanations. Enactment, ongoing service delivery, attribution and independent coding remain separate.

The same platform, in **Supporting Working Families**, pledges a significant increase in the **Child and Dependent Care Tax Credit**. [Public Law 117-2](https://www.govinfo.gov/app/details/PLAW-117publ2), approved **11 March 2021**, section 9631, enacted an expansion **for tax year 2021 only**. [IRS guidance dated 2 March 2022](https://www.irs.gov/newsroom/understanding-the-child-and-dependent-care-credit) confirms expanded eligible expenses, $8,000 for one qualifying person or $16,000 for two or more, with credit-rate/eligibility restrictions. The temporary nature must be evaluated separately from enactment; neither partial nor full fulfillment is assigned.

### US 2024, Canada 2025, and UK 2024 implementation chain

[Public Law 119-1](https://www.govinfo.gov/app/details/PLAW-119publ1), approved **29 January 2025**, is indexed as an **unmatched** immigration implementation event. It does not establish that any broad 2024 platform immigration commitment was fully delivered.

Canada's [Bill C-15 royal-assent text](https://www.parl.ca/DocumentViewer/en/45-1/bill/C-15/royal-assent) confirms **26 March 2026** enactment; section 200 authorizes payments of **up to C$11.5 billion** for Build Canada Homes. This is an **authorization ceiling**, not actual payments, housing completions, or fulfillment of the April 2025 Liberal platform. The existing [Canada Strong pre-election source](../../data/promises/primary-crossnational-discovery-2026-10-30.json) remains unscored.

The previously indexed [Great British Energy Act 2025](https://www.legislation.gov.uk/ukpga/2025/16) came into force on Royal Assent, **15 May 2025**, under section 10(2). Its section 5 creates a separate six-month strategic-priorities duty; enactment and commencement are not evidence that operational outcomes or energy benefits were delivered. No duplicate event is added to the data file.

### First actual Canadian fiscal rows (historical baseline only)

The official [Housing, Infrastructure and Communities Canada 2024–25 Departmental Results Report](https://housing-infrastructure.canada.ca/pub/drr-rrm/2025/2025-drr-rrm-01-eng.html), **Table 10 / Total / authorities used**, reports actual spending including internal services of **C$6,828,959,017 (FY2022–23)**, **C$7,619,725,159 (FY2023–24)** and **C$7,968,878,253 (FY2024–25)**. These are **three departmental annual observations**, not three general-government fiscal years, quarterly expenditure points, or capital-spending shares. The report's **Graph 1** instead shows **C$7,619,725,160** for FY2023–24: a **C$1 discrepancy** retained explicitly for independent reconciliation. The 2024–25 year ends **31 March 2025**, before Canada's April 2025 federal election, so none of these actuals measures delivery of the 2025 Build Canada Homes commitment.
