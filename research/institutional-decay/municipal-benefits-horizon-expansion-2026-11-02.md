# Institutional decay — South Africa, Netherlands and UK evidence expansion (2 November 2026)

**Scientific status:** additive, source-verified descriptive research; no causal identification, representative experimental result, personal criminal-guilt inference or cross-country institutional-decay ranking. Dataset: [municipal-benefits-horizon-evidence-2026-11-02.json](../../data/institutional-decay/municipal-benefits-horizon-evidence-2026-11-02.json). **12 source records, 28 structured observations and 10 dated accountability/audit timeline events.**

## Verified findings and disconfirming evidence

**South Africa — local-government audit results (FY2023–24).** Auditor-General of South Africa (AGSA) lists 257 municipalities: 41 clean audit opinions, 99 unqualified with findings, 90 qualified, six adverse, 11 disclaimed, and ten outstanding at the 31 January 2025 cutoff. The six categories reconcile to 257; completed audits total 247. Among **247 completed audits**, 206 had material compliance findings and 119 had material performance-report findings. The AGSA also reports 59 improved and 40 regressed audit outcomes since 2020–21; a separate government account reports 34 clean municipal audits for FY2022–23, versus 41 in FY2023–24. These changes disprove any assumption that *every* audit outcome worsened. Audit findings are not criminal verdicts and the AGSA explicitly cautions that its work does not directly measure service delivery. The overview mixes rounded headline percentages and different denominators: **use the exact category counts, the 257 total and the 247 completed audit cutoff, not a mechanically pooled percentage**. Sources: [AGSA audit overview](https://mfma-2024.agsareports.co.za/pages/overview), [AGSA full category table](https://mfma-2024.agsa.co.za/), [official 2022–23 audit account](https://www.sanews.gov.za/node/70433), and [29 May 2025 parliamentary briefing record](https://www.parliament.gov.za/storage/app/media/Docs/atc/01ls62wgf6nxa34jdwlvgl23udihk7bocd.pdf).

**Netherlands — childcare-benefits scandal, differing accountability channels.** On 17 December 2020, the Dutch parliamentary inquiry reported fundamental rule-of-law failures in benefits-fraud enforcement. On 7 January 2021 the Dutch Public Prosecution Service announced it would **not** open a criminal investigation on the allegations it assessed; that is a prosecutorial screening decision, not a judicial acquittal, nor a reversal of the parliamentary finding. The Rutte III cabinet resigned on 15 January 2021, a political accountability outcome rather than a criminal sentence. Official 18 September 2023 administrative matching identified **1,819 children of 1,168 affected parents** with care placements; the match alone **does not establish that the benefits scandal caused any individual care placement**. The Dutch government subsequently acknowledged compounded harms in its 30 June 2025 response to an independent report. Sources: [Parliamentary inquiry release](https://www.tweedekamer.nl/nieuws/kamernieuws/eindverslag-onderzoek-kinderopvangtoeslag-overhandigd), [prosecutor's decision](https://www.om.nl/actueel/nieuws/2021/01/07/geen-strafrechtelijk-onderzoek-naar-belastingdienst), [prime minister's statement](https://www.rijksoverheid.nl/documenten/2021/01/15/verklaring-van-minister-president-mark-rutte-over-het-aftreden-van-het-kabinet), [2023 matching announcement](https://www.government.nl/latest/news/2023/09/18/targeted-support-offered-to-victims-of-the-childcare-benefits-scandal-with-children-taken-into-care), and [2025 government response](https://www.government.nl/latest/news/2025/06/30/government-acknowledges-additional-suffering-of-children-placed-in-care).

**Netherlands — documented redress progress and limits.** A 4 July 2025 government progress release reported approximately **69,400** potentially affected registrants, approximately **42,000** confirmed affected parents, **more than 64,000** completed integrated compensation assessments and a separately published **92.5%** completion rate. The counts are rounded and refer to different populations/statuses. Do **not** derive the published percentage from the rounded figures or claim that completed assessments equal fully restored families. Source: [official remediation progress release](https://www.rijksoverheid.nl/actueel/nieuws/2025/07/04/gedupeerde-ouders-krijgen-keuze-uit-twee-routes-voor-aanvullende-schade).

**United Kingdom — Horizon accountability and remediation.** On 9 June 2025 the Department for Business and Trade reported **£1,039 million** cumulative compensation paid to **more than 7,300** postmasters; **£245 million** from the Horizon Convictions Redress Scheme is **part of**, not additional to, the cumulative total. On 8 July 2025 the statutory Horizon Inquiry published Volume 1 with **19 urgent recommendations**; it reports approximately 225 hearing days, 298 witnesses and approximately 274,600 documents disclosed to Core Participants through 6 June 2025. The inquiry reported failings in redress, including inadequate offers in some cases. Recommendations are not implemented sanctions or criminal convictions; compensation disbursement is not proof every claim was resolved. Sources: [UK government payment release](https://www.gov.uk/government/news/compensation-to-postmasters-reaches-1-billion-milestone) and [independent inquiry report release](https://www.postofficehorizoninquiry.org.uk/news/chair-calls-swift-action-deliver-full-and-fair-compensation-urgent-report).

## Permissions and provenance

- **AGSA:** [Copyright and disclaimer](https://www.agsa.co.za/who-we-are/copyright-and-disclaimer) explicitly allows applying published data with acknowledgment of AGSA as source and disclosure that the derivative analysis is ours. Its separate [terms](https://www.agsa.co.za/terms-of-use) retain copyright; no tables, graphics, logos, or bulk documents are copied.
- **Netherlands:** [Rijksoverheid](https://www.rijksoverheid.nl/service/copyright), [Government.nl](https://www.government.nl/service/copyright) and the [Public Prosecution Service](https://www.om.nl/service/copyright) generally apply CC0 to website text, with exclusions for specially protected content and imagery. Dutch parliamentary website page-specific reuse is not independently verified; only short public factual statements are stored.
- **United Kingdom:** [GOV.UK terms](https://www.gov.uk/help/terms-conditions) generally provide OGL v3 reuse with attribution and exceptions; the independent Horizon Inquiry site's specific licence was not confirmed. For the latter only brief factual observations are stored, no expressive narrative or media.
- **South African parliamentary PDF and SAnews:** page-specific licences not independently confirmed. Only dated public procedural facts or simple counts, no protected narrative.
- All records contain exact official source URLs, source publication dates, claim classifications, denominators, status caveats and source-permission notes. No personal data, paywalled content, synthetic survey results or imputed outcomes.

## Validation and limitations

The dataset is schema-aligned with earlier `accountability-service-cases.v1` expansions. Its six AGSA audit categories sum to **257**; **247 completed + 10 outstanding = 257**. Independent stdlib regression tests live in [test_institutional_decay_sa_nl_uk.py](../../tests/test_institutional_decay_sa_nl_uk.py) and a dedicated [GitHub Actions workflow](../../.github/workflows/validate-institutional-decay-sa-nl-uk.yml). Tests cover source provenance, uniqueness, event dates, precision, distinct denominators, causal-status guardrails, and disjoint accountability dispositions.

**Scientific blockers remain:** no recruited/consented representative factorial experiment; no sufficient individual-level trust linkage or identified causal panel. These are genuine missing research inputs, not reasons to invent causal estimates. The separate research-only dataset does **not** modify `data/app-feed.json`, redistricting APIs or production UX. A future product ingestion requires explicit compatibility, source-rights and concept-mapping checks.
