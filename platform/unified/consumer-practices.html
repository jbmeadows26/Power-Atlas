How the extra cost gets hidden

Most tactics exploit a gap between the first promise and the final obligation. Here's the pattern in plain English.

01

The attractive headline

A low price, “unlimited” service, big upgrade discount or easy cancellation gets your attention.

02

The catch emerges later

Mandatory fees, shared allowances, recurring charges, early-exit penalties or extra steps change the true bargain.

03

The consumer absorbs the cost

You spend more money, time, or effort—or discover that the promised offer cannot be combined as expected.

Four common patterns

01

Hidden final price

Fees or register charges differ from the upfront offer.

02

Subscription traps

Joining is easy; canceling or avoiding renewal isn't.

03

Restricted promotions

The advertised benefit is limited by account-wide rules or qualification requirements.

04

Broken promises

Delivery, refunds, service quality or product terms allegedly differ from what was promised.

Important distinction: A regulator's allegation is not a court finding. A settlement often resolves claims without admitting wrongdoing. T-Mobile's shared-Pick rules are documented terms, but whether they constitute consumer deception is not established.

What this evidence can—and cannot—show

Documented

Regulatory actions, court-entered orders, agency-announced settlements, publicly disclosed terms and dated refund activity.

Still to investigate

Whether an allegation is proven, how frequently the same practice occurs today, what each household actually paid, and whether a particular actor intended to mislead.
