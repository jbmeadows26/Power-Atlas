Federal election-cycle directory

Every two-year federal election cycle has congressional elections. Presidential cycles additionally elect a president. Primaries and special elections fall within the cycle totals rather than appearing as separate nationwide totals.

State and local coverage: Governors, state legislatures, judicial elections, ballot measures, municipalities and many off-year elections are not covered by federal FEC totals. They require separate state/local disclosure records. These categories are recorded as coverage gaps, not silently merged into federal figures. Explore state-level finance data.

Sources, caveats and audit trail

What does “all election cycles” mean here?

Federal two-year cycle coverage is 2002 through 2026. All 13 have the FEC's comparable congressional first-18-month benchmarks; 12 have full 24-month congressional totals. The 2026 FEC statistics here end June 30, 2026, although the separately sourced Brennan donor extract ends October 14. We have not loaded pre-2002 federal history, 2028 filings, a comprehensive donor ledger or all 50 states' campaign-finance transactions.

What's being measured?

Congressional and presidential figures are candidate committee receipts. PAC receipts are what registered federal PACs report collecting. Party receipts are federal political-party committee funds. Independent expenditures are separate outlays supporting or opposing candidates without coordinating with their campaigns. None of these categories is a substitute for a transaction-level accounting of donors to recipients and spending.

Why are some donor years missing?

The Brennan Center's top-40 comparison covers only 2014, 2018, 2022 and 2026. OpenSecrets provides the additional partial extracts displayed for 2020 and 2024. Other federal cycles have FEC aggregate numbers here but no comparable donor ranking yet. Missing lists do not mean there were no large donors.
