03 / FOLLOW THE POWER

Why might one place cost more?

These are evidence-backed mechanisms to investigate—not a statistical attribution of the difference above.

01

Housing supply and land use

Construction limits, local zoning, access to employment and demand affect the available housing stock.

Decision-makers: planning boards, local elected officials, developers and landowners.

02

Ownership and finance

Landlords, investors, lenders and borrowing conditions can change the incentives behind pricing.

Who may benefit: owners collecting rent or financing revenue. A particular local effect requires separate evidence.

03

Public decisions and essential services

Taxes, insurance, utilities and transportation shape total household burdens; none is automatically explained by a rent benchmark.

Who pays: households facing the charges. Follow the source and actual rate structure before assigning responsibility.

Investigate transportation →
Evidence rule: A ZIP-level rent gap is a measured difference. It is not proof that a named corporation, politician, regulation or investor caused that gap. Source-backed connections and unresolved leads remain visibly separate.

Sources and limits

Housing: FY2027 HUD Small Area Fair Market Rent (SAFMR) gross-rent references, 0–4 bedrooms; effective October 1, 2026 unless an area has a current-year exception. HUD references are used in housing-assistance programs and are not apartment listings, the median rent residents currently pay, or a guarantee of voucher payment. The ZIP records in this feature are a CC BY 4.0 compilation by Multifamily Today of HUD-derived data, not a direct HUD API. The page labels any FY2026 carryover where supplied.

Other expenses: nationally or regionally reported measures are not presented as precise ZIP-level prices. Where consistent comparisons are missing, the feature explains what is missing rather than calculating a fictional total.
