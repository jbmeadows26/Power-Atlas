What “dark money” does—and doesn't—tell us

Dark money broadly describes election-related spending where the public cannot identify the original funders. Nonprofit filings can reveal grants made out, but many 501(c)(4) and 501(c)(6) organizations do not publicly disclose their donor names. An IRS Schedule I grant and a committee receipt may suggest a connection, but they cannot be matched without supporting evidence.

A shared political consultant, registered agent, mailing address or similar LLC name is an investigative clue—not proof of common ownership, coordination, illegality or hidden donor identity. The tracker deliberately does not publish private addresses or create unreviewed person-to-person links.
