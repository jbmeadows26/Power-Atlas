What these numbers prove—and what they don't

This is a sourced donor leaderboard, not an assertion that any donor purchased a vote, directed a candidate, or caused a policy outcome. Recipient-level money flows and specific policy connections are a separate research stage.

Source and methodology

Original: Brennan Center, Big Changes to Makeup of the Biggest Midterm Donors, updated October 29, 2026. Published data source (four CSV tables). Data used under attribution; source descriptions and donor grouping are preserved. 2026 transactions run through October 14.

  • Each row is a donor or donor grouping; couples and companies with principals may appear together.
  • Donors outside the top 40 are absent, not zero-dollar donors.
  • Party labels, sectors, and dark-money flags are the publisher's researched classifications, not independent Power Atlas determinations.
  • Top-40 dollar totals are contributions to candidates, parties, and committees; they are not the same as independent expenditures, ad spending, or final election costs.
  • “Not flagged” does not certify full transparency. “Both parties” cannot be split proportionally without transaction-level evidence.
  • Multiple-year figures are in nominal USD; use price-adjusted figures for real purchasing-power comparisons.

Coverage: curated reference dataset, 160 donor-cycle records. Not yet a recipient-by-recipient transaction ledger. Explore official FEC campaign-finance data or inspect the exact dashboard dataset.
