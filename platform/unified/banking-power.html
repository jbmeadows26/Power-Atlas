01 / THE CRASHES

Pick a crisis. Trace the outcome.

Not every rescue is a taxpayer loss. Not every protected depositor is a rescued shareholder. Tap a chapter and compare who was helped, who lost and what legal accountability is documented.

Crises use different definitions and accounting bases. Amounts are nominal historical U.S. dollars and must not be added together. “Benefit” does not necessarily mean net profit; an obligation paid can also be a recovery of an existing claim.

Why this matters: payment received is not the same as profit

AIG’s counterparties had contractual claims and collateral arrangements. The rescue influenced the settlement and recovery of those exposures. To claim an institution earned a specific windfall, we would also need contract value, hedges, replacement cost and the counterfactual loss without support. GAO and New York Fed records describe the decisions and controversies.

FOLLOW THE PROFIT / 2023 BANK ACQUISITIONS

Some banks failed. Others booked gains.

Two acquirers reported bargain-purchase gains after buying operations from failed banks. A financial-statement gain means acquired net assets were valued above the purchase price—not that Treasury paid that amount in cash.

Does an accounting gain mean a bank “profited from a bailout”?

Not by itself. The buyer reported an acquisition-date valuation gain. Its ultimate economic return also depends on future loan losses, integration costs, deposit retention, financing, shared-loss agreements, taxes and future earnings. Similarly, the FDIC's receivership cost is an estimate that can change. The $9.81B and $2.9B figures use different tax and reporting treatments and should not be added together as a single bailout profit total.

Who else received money but may not have profited?

AIG's 2008 support involved counterparties including Goldman Sachs ($12.9B), Société Générale ($11.9B) and Deutsche Bank ($11.8B) in gross payments or collateral flows. Congressional oversight documented these flows, but did not establish that each amount was a net windfall. The accounting baseline is different from a bargain-purchase gain or an FDIC loss.

03 / THE ACCOUNTABILITY LEDGER

Fines aren't prison sentences.

Compare actual government findings, settlement amounts, personal sanctions and criminal outcomes. A company paying a settlement is not proof that an executive was convicted.

● Company civil resolution● Personal regulatory action● Criminal case
How we judge “held liable” — and why “nobody was punished” is misleading

Each case must separate an allegation, official finding, settlement, guilty plea, criminal conviction, restitution, corporate penalty and personal sanction. A deferred prosecution agreement is not equivalent to a jury conviction. No action found in one source is not proof that no action ever happened. An updated person-by-person docket is needed for any claim of complete non-prosecution.

POLICY CASE / 2024–2025

The overdraft rule that never took effect

The CFPB's December 2024 rule would have required very large banks to cap overdraft fees at $5, use a justified cost-based charge, or treat the product like credit with additional disclosures. Congress disapproved the rule in 2025. It has no force or effect.

The vote and legal change are documented. Whether a particular bank's political spending caused any vote requires separate evidence.

1. Who paid?

PAC committees, individuals, executives, trade groups and independent-expenditure organizations are legally distinct.

2. What was requested?

Lobbying disclosures identify clients and topics, but may not reveal every specific request.

3. What changed?

Match bills, amendments, roll calls, agency rules and outcomes—without confusing proximity for causation.

Federal campaigns generally cannot accept bank corporate-treasury contributions directly. Separate PAC, independent spending and individual contributions must not be merged or mislabeled.
